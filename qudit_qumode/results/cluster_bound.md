# Cluster speedup check

Generated by `scripts/split_benchmark.py` at commit d98477d.

## Assumptions

a. Gate times are class means at N_c = 3, a model approximation.
b. The link-modulated term costs one MS window on the qubit side, an assumption of the timing model.
c. Hub placement is optimal, A shares its ion with K-1 neighbours. Star placement only.
d. Chain placement takes nearest-neighbour links only, the 1D array of N chromophores of Ref. vu2025 Sec. 2.4 and Eq. 52, with K consecutive chromophores per ion.

## 5b. Chain placement, termwise from Vu Eq. 18 to 21

Each chromophore xi carries a high-frequency mode xi_0 and a low-frequency mode xi_1 (Vu Sec. 2.1.4). Bond i joins sites i and i+1 and collects, from H_2^(xi=i) of Eq. 21: terms 2 and 3, two mode-independent nearest-neighbour electronic exchanges; and terms 4 and 5, two mode-dependent vibronic link terms, the first modulated by i_1 and the second by (i+1)_1.

Eq. 52 drops the boundary low-frequency modes, assuming negligible low-frequency modes at the boundaries. Here those are 1_1 and N_c_1. A link term whose mode is dropped goes with it, so each END bond keeps one link term and each INTERIOR bond keeps two. Surviving modes: N_c high-frequency plus N_c - 2 low-frequency = 2N_c - 2, the count of Eq. 52 and of mas.tex:1039.

### 1. Every term of H_2 for N_c = 4, bond by bond

Chromophore low-frequency modes 1_1, 2_1, 3_1, 4_1. Eq. 52 drops **1_1** and **4_1** (the boundary chromophores 1 and 4), leaving 2_1 and 3_1. Surviving modes: 4 high-frequency (1_0..4_0) + 2 low-frequency (2_1, 3_1) = 6 = 2N_c - 2.

| bond | sites | kind | exchange terms (Eq. 21 terms 2,3) | link terms as written (terms 4,5) | modes those would use | dropped | link terms kept | mode(s) kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (1,2) | end | 2 | 2 | 1_1, 2_1 | 1_1 | 1 | 2_1 |
| 2 | (2,3) | interior | 2 | 2 | 2_1, 3_1 | -- | 2 | 2_1, 3_1 |
| 3 | (3,4) | end | 2 | 2 | 3_1, 4_1 | 4_1 | 1 | 3_1 |

The site term of Eq. 21, term 1, couples xi_1 to its own electronic state and is not a bond term. It too disappears for the boundary chromophores, leaving 2 of 4 site terms at N_c = 4 (modes 2_1 and 3_1). It does not enter c_in or c_out.

Totals at N_c = 4: 6 exchange terms = 2(N_c-1) = 6, and 4 link terms = 2(N_c-2) = 4. Without the boundary rule there would be 2(N_c-1) = 6 link terms; the end bonds lose one each.

### 3b. Large-N_c limit, recomputed

Almost every bond is interior as N_c grows, so the per-bond figures that survive are c_in = 1.9629 us and c_out = 1200 us = 6 t_MS.

**Window-dominated.** sum_all c_out = 2 end at 5 t_MS + (N_c-3) interior at 6 t_MS = (6 N_c - 8) t_MS, and the crossing bonds are interior for large N_c, so

    L_chain = (6 N_c - 8) / (6 (ceil(N_c/K) - 1))  ->  K

still K, as in the one-link model. The per-bond window cost cancels whatever its value: the numerator grows as (window cost) x N_c and the denominator as (window cost) x N_c/K, so only the block size survives. Raising the interior bond from 5 to 6 windows changes the finite-N_c values but not the limit. The star's counterpart (N_c-1)/(N_c-K) still tends to 1.

**Full limit.** Taking slopes in N_c, with r, cr, cd, czd the class means:

    lim S_chain = [2r + 2cr + 4cd + 6 t_MS]
                / [2r + 2cr + 2czd + (1 - 1/K)(2 t_X,loc + 2 t_L,loc) + 6 t_MS / K]

It still sits below K for every K, and still above 1, so the conclusion is unchanged -- a chain keeps a constant-factor speedup as the molecule grows and a star keeps none.
