## What the numbers actually show

**Qudit total gates**: 36 at Nc=3 -> 135 at Nc=12, exactly `11*Nc + 3` (slope 11.0).  **One-hot total gates**: 32 -> 131, exactly `11*Nc - 1` (slope 11.0).  The qudit sits `+4` above one-hot at *every* Nc -- a pure constant, not a scaling advantage.  

**Entangling gates are literally IDENTICAL** between qudit and one-hot: both `7*Nc - 1` (83 at Nc=12).  The four-gate total-count gap is entirely non-entangling (mode Rotation + qps bookkeeping).  In the metric that actually sets error budget -- two-body gate count -- qudit and one-hot in this vibronic model are **the same**.  

**Compact (log2 qubit) CNOTs**: 58 at Nc=3, 2242 at Nc=12, so 27.0x the qudit entangling-gate count at Nc=12.  The compact curve jumps in ratchets whenever `m = ceil(log2(Nc+1))` increments (Nc=4: m 2->3 jumps CNOTs 58->250; Nc=8: m 3->4 jumps CNOTs 448->1494).  Between ratchets the growth is linear at fixed m.  The pairwise `C^(m-1) Ry` decomposition and the per-site MCPhase both scale with `m` and dominate the CNOT budget.  

**BLUNT VERDICT.**  In native-ISA units, qudit vs one-hot for this A-star single-excitation vibronic model is a wash: identical entangling counts, +4 constant on totals, forever.  The qudit advantage in the *simulation* is not "fewer gates than one-hot"; it is register-footprint (1 ion vs Nc qubits), no MS-gate shuttling between ions, and no need for the ancilla / decoding infrastructure that any compact encoding demands.  The qudit vs qubit gap that IS large (27.0x at Nc=12) exists only against the compact log2 encoding, which no reasonable qubit implementation of a single-excitation model would actually pick.  Reporting "qudit crushes qubits" without saying which qubit encoding is being crushed is misleading.
