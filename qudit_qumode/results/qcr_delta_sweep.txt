QCR DISPERSIVE-DETUNING SWEEP -- defending delta_disp = 40 kHz
====================================================================================================================
  Omega_sb/2pi = 4.000 kHz (MEASURED, Meth)
  nu/2pi       = 770 kHz (MEASURED, Meth) -- stand-in ceiling, see below
  T_QCR at delta = 40 kHz = 55.93 us (read from appendix_table.md)

  delta_disp is OUR CHOICE, not a Meth parameter.  Meth drives resonant
  sidebands (delta = 0) and never uses a dispersive detuning.  The two
  limits that bracket a sensible value:
    lower: delta >> Omega_sb = 4 kHz (dispersive validity)
    upper: delta << gap to the nearest motional mode (spectral selectivity)
  CAVEAT: the upper limit is the MODE SPACING, not nu.  A 3-ion chain has
  3 axial modes with gaps smaller than nu = 770 kHz.  This repo does not
  anchor the mode spectrum, so nu is used as a stand-in and the real
  ceiling is tighter.  Flagged, not resolved.

  T_QCR scales LINEARLY with delta because chi = Omega_sb^2/(4 delta) and
  the gate angle is chi*T.  So T(delta) = T_ref * delta/delta_ref.

  READING
    The sequential ratio swings from 10.48x at 20 kHz to 3.18x at 80 kHz.
    Smaller delta makes the gate FASTER and the ratio BETTER, but the
    Schrieffer-Wolff parameter Omega_sb/delta grows and the SW infidelity
    and leakage grow with it -- read the 1-F and leakage columns before
    treating a small delta as free.  That is why both are tabulated here.
    At the chosen 40 kHz: Omega/delta = 0.100, 1-F(fit+VZ) = 2.840325e-03, ratio seq = [5.73x, 6.04x].

  NO ADOPTION.  runtime_table.md keeps delta = 40 kHz and T_QCR = 55.93 us.
  This run turns that from an assumption into a bracketed, reasoned choice,
  and shows the cost of moving it in either direction.
