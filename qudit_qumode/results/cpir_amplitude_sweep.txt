========================================================================================================
INDEPENDENCE CHECK -- was every row simulated at its own delta?
========================================================================================================
  Every quantity this table reports is scale invariant: delta*T =
  2 pi and Omega_sb/delta = sqrt(2 theta/pi) are both free of delta,
  so the leakage column is identical at every row BY CONSTRUCTION.
  An identical column is also what a scaled copy of the 40 kHz row
  would produce, so the column alone cannot distinguish the two.

  The probe below breaks the invariance on purpose.  It evaluates
  the propagator at the same ABSOLUTE time, 5.000 us, for every
  row.  That is a different fraction of the detuning cycle at each
  delta, so a scaled copy could not reproduce it; only a genuine
  propagation at that delta can.

      delta   delta (rad/us)    T (us)  T*delta/2pi   P(g) at t=5 us
         20         0.125664    50.000       1.0000     7.060843e-03
         30         0.188496    33.333       1.0000     1.519258e-02
         40         0.251327    25.000       1.0000     2.535357e-02
         60         0.376991    16.667       1.0000     4.739145e-02
         80         0.502655    12.500       1.0000     6.415500e-02

  The probe spans a factor of 9.1 across the five rows.
  CONFIRMED: each row is an independent propagation at its own
  delta.  No row is scaled from the 40 kHz result.

  Mechanically, set_delta() rewrites QC.DELTA_DISP, and
  AC.build_H_amp() reads QC.DELTA_DISP at call time into the drive's
  args dict, so each solve and each measure builds a fresh
  Hamiltonian at the current detuning.  The table above is the
  behavioural confirmation of that.

========================================================================================================
READING
========================================================================================================
  The sweep REVERSES relative to the published one.  T = 2*pi/delta,
  so a smaller detuning now means a LONGER gate, not a shorter one,
  and the ratio rises with delta instead of falling.  The published
  table had the opposite slope because it priced T = theta/chi.

  Leakage stays below 3e-03 at every detuning.

  The amplitude required grows linearly with delta: theta = chi T
  with chi = Omega_sb^2/(4 delta) and T = 2*pi/delta gives
  Omega_sb = delta sqrt(2 theta/pi).  That closed form is the
  leading-order Schrieffer-Wolff estimate only; the tabulated
  amplitudes are solved numerically under the full drive at each
  detuning and run about 2% above it.  Across the sweep that is
  2.06 to 8.23 kHz against a measured 4.0 kHz, so the far ends of the
  sweep ask for a drive well away from the characterised point.
  Omega_sb/delta stays near 0.10 throughout, so the drive remains
  dispersive by the same margin at every detuning.
